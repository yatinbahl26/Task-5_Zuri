## Universal Answering Frameworks

Use the appropriate framework depending on the question.

### 1\. INVESTIGATE – Technical AML Scenarios

Use this framework when answering transaction monitoring and financial crime investigation questions.

| Letter | Meaning | What to Explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Identify | Understand the alert and trigger |
| N | Know | Review the customer and expected activity |
| V | View | Analyse transaction flows, values, frequency, counterparties, and geography |
| E | Examine | Identify red flags and inconsistencies |
| S | Seek | Obtain reliable evidence and supporting documents |
| T | Test | Assess the customer's explanation |
| I | Identify | Decide whether to close, request information, or escalate |
| GATE | Ground and record | Support the decision with evidence and documentation |

### 2\. STAR – Behavioural and Experience Questions

Use STAR when discussing your professional experience.

- **S – Situation:** Briefly explain the context.
- **T – Task:** Describe your responsibility.
- **A – Action:** Explain what you personally did. This should be the longest part.
- **R – Result:** Explain the actual outcome and what you learned.

Use genuine examples and real performance metrics only.

### 3\. PREP – Knowledge and Opinion Questions

Use PREP for direct knowledge-based or opinion questions.

- **P – Point:** Answer the question directly.
- **R – Reason:** Explain why.
- **E – Example:** Provide a relevant example.
- **P – Point:** Reinforce your conclusion.

### When Facts Are Incomplete

If an interviewer provides incomplete information:

1. Explain what is currently known.
2. Identify the specific missing information.
3. Explain how you would obtain or verify it.
4. State that you would not close the alert until material gaps are resolved.

**Avoid guessing or making assumptions.**

# PART I – CORE AML AND TRANSACTION MONITORING

**Focus:** AML fundamentals, investigation methodology, sanctions, PEP, regulations, practical cases, Client ABC assessment, SAR narrative, and financial markets.

## Section 1 – AML Fundamentals

### Q1. What is Anti-Money Laundering (AML)?

AML refers to laws, regulations, policies, and procedures designed to prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate money.

The three commonly described stages are:

1. **Placement:** Introducing illicit funds into the financial system.
2. **Layering:** Moving funds through transactions to obscure their origin.
3. **Integration:** Making funds appear legitimate through investments or purchases.

### Q2. What is Transaction Monitoring?

Transaction Monitoring is the review of customer transactions to identify unusual or potentially suspicious activity.

Systems generate alerts using rules, thresholds, customer profiles, and behavioural patterns.

Analysts review customer information, transaction history, counterparties, and geographical exposure before documenting their findings and deciding whether to close or escalate an alert.

### Q3. What is the difference between an unusual and suspicious transaction?

An unusual transaction differs from the customer's expected or historical behaviour.

A suspicious transaction is one where available information creates grounds for concern about possible money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, or another financial crime.

An unusual transaction requires investigation but is not automatically suspicious.

### Q4. What are the three stages of money laundering?

- Placement
- Layering
- Integration

### Q5. What is a Risk-Based Approach (RBA)?

A Risk-Based Approach means applying controls proportionate to the risks presented by the customer, product, transaction, geography, and business relationship.

Higher-risk relationships may require enhanced due diligence, more frequent reviews, and closer monitoring.

### Q6. What is the difference between CDD and EDD?

**CDD:** Identifying and verifying customers, understanding the purpose of the relationship, and assessing expected activity.

**EDD:** Additional measures for higher-risk customers, including deeper source-of-wealth and source-of-funds checks, additional documentation, and enhanced monitoring.

### Q7. What is the difference between Source of Funds and Source of Wealth?

- **Source of Funds:** Explains where the money for a particular transaction originated.
- **Source of Wealth:** Explains how the customer accumulated their overall wealth.

Examples include salary, property sales, inheritance, business ownership, and investment proceeds.

### Q8. What is a Beneficial Owner?

A beneficial owner is the natural person who ultimately owns or controls a customer or legal entity, even when ownership is held through intermediaries.

### Q9. What is the difference between KYC and AML?

KYC focuses on identifying and verifying customers.

AML is the broader framework that includes KYC, transaction monitoring, sanctions controls, suspicious activity reporting, and financial crime prevention.

### Q10. What is a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR)?

A SAR is a report submitted to the relevant Financial Intelligence Unit or competent authority when the applicable reporting threshold is met.

It typically includes the subject, account details, suspicious activity, transaction dates, amounts, counterparties, investigation findings, and reasons for suspicion.

## Section 2 – Transaction Monitoring Investigation

### Q11. Explain your end-to-end transaction monitoring investigation process.

1. Understand the alert trigger and monitoring rule.
2. Review the customer's profile and KYC information.
3. Analyse incoming and outgoing transactions.
4. Identify unusual patterns and red flags.
5. Conduct internal checks, screening, and appropriate research.
6. Review customer explanations and supporting documents.
7. Decide whether to close, request information, or escalate.
8. Document the investigation and rationale.

### Q12. What red flags do you look for?

Common indicators include:

- Transactions inconsistent with declared income.
- Sudden changes in transaction behaviour.
- Unexplained third-party funding.
- Rapid movement of funds.
- Complex or circular transfers.
- Structuring.
- High-risk geographical exposure.
- Transactions without an apparent economic purpose.
- Potential sanctions connections.

A red flag is an indicator requiring assessment, not proof of financial crime.

### Q13. How do you determine whether transactions are consistent with a customer's profile?

Compare actual activity with occupation, income, wealth, account purpose, historical activity, counterparties, and geography.

Investigate material differences by seeking credible explanations and appropriate evidence.

### Q14. What would you do if a customer did not respond to an RFI?

Follow the approved follow-up process, document the request and attempts, assess available information, and escalate if material concerns remain unresolved.

Non-response alone does not prove money laundering.

### Q15. What is Structuring or Smurfing?

Structuring involves splitting transactions into smaller amounts to evade controls or reporting requirements.

Smurfing involves using multiple people or accounts to conduct smaller transactions to conceal the true source or ownership of funds.

### Q16. What is a False Positive?

A false positive is an alert that, after appropriate investigation, is determined not to indicate suspicious activity.

Closure still requires documented reasoning and compliance with internal procedures.

### Q17. What is the difference between Alert Closure and Escalation?

**Closure:** Appropriate when investigation establishes a reasonable explanation and no unresolved concern remains.

**Escalation:** Appropriate when material red flags remain unexplained or additional review is required.

Escalation does not automatically mean a SAR/STR must be filed.

### Q18. How would you investigate a large incoming international transfer?

Review:

- Sender identity and relationship.
- Origin country.
- Amount and payment purpose.
- Source of funds.
- Account ownership.
- Historical activity.
- Counterparties.
- Applicable sanctions and geographical risks.

### Q19. What is the difference between Velocity and Volume Monitoring?

- **Velocity:** Identifies unusual transaction frequency or rapid movement.
- **Volume:** Identifies unusually high transaction values or aggregate amounts.

### Q20. How would you investigate rapid movement of funds?

Review incoming and outgoing transactions, timing, balances, counterparties, destinations, purposes, and customer profile.

Determine whether the account is functioning as a pass-through account.

## Section 3 – Sanctions, PEP and Adverse Media

### Q21. What are Financial Sanctions?

Financial sanctions are restrictive measures imposed by governments or international authorities against individuals, entities, organisations, or countries.

Measures may include asset freezes and restrictions on providing funds or financial services.

### Q22. What is the OFAC SDN List?

The Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons List is maintained by the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control.

It identifies individuals and entities subject to specific US sanctions.

### Q23. What would you do if a customer matched a sanctions screening alert?

- Review the full name and aliases.
- Compare date of birth and nationality.
- Check addresses and identification numbers.
- Review associated entities.
- Determine whether the match is false or potentially genuine.
- Follow sanctions escalation procedures immediately.

### Q24. What is a Politically Exposed Person (PEP)?

A PEP is an individual who holds or has held a prominent public function and may present heightened bribery or corruption risk.

PEP status is not evidence of wrongdoing but generally requires risk-based enhanced due diligence.

### Q25. What is Adverse Media Screening?

Adverse media screening reviews credible public information concerning allegations or reporting related to financial crime, fraud, corruption, sanctions violations, tax evasion, or other relevant misconduct.

### Q26. What is Anti-Bribery and Corruption (ABC)?

ABC controls are designed to prevent bribery, improper payments, and abuse of authority.

Relevant legislation includes:

- US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA).
- UK Bribery Act 2010.

### Q27. What is the difference between a PEP and a Sanctioned Individual?

PEP status relates to a prominent public function and associated corruption risks.

Sanctions status arises from restrictive measures imposed under an applicable sanctions regime.

A PEP is not automatically sanctioned.

## Section 4 – Regulations and Compliance

### Q28. What is FATF?

The Financial Action Task Force develops international standards to combat:

- Money laundering.
- Terrorist financing.
- Proliferation financing.

### Q29. What is the difference between FATF Grey-Listed and Black-Listed Jurisdictions?

- **Grey-listed:** Jurisdictions under increased monitoring.
- **Black-listed:** Common terminology for jurisdictions subject to a FATF call for action.

These designations inform risk assessments but do not automatically make every connected customer or transaction suspicious.

### Q30. What AML regulations should financial institutions understand?

Examples include:

- US Bank Secrecy Act.
- USA PATRIOT Act.
- FinCEN requirements.
- UK Money Laundering Regulations.
- Proceeds of Crime Act 2002.
- Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018.
- EU AML framework.
- FATF Recommendations.

Applicable requirements depend on jurisdiction and business activity.

### Q31. What is the difference between Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing?

Money laundering disguises criminal proceeds to make them appear legitimate.

Terrorist financing involves providing or collecting funds to support terrorist activity or organisations.

Terrorist financing can involve legitimate or illegitimate sources of funds.

### Q32. What is Tipping Off?

Tipping off means disclosing information about a suspicious activity report or investigation where such disclosure is prohibited.

Employees must maintain confidentiality and follow internal procedures.

## Section 5 – Practical Investigation Scenarios

### Q33. A customer earning $5,000 monthly receives $100,000 from an unrelated individual. What would you do?

Investigate the sender, relationship, payment purpose, source of funds, supporting evidence, and historical activity.

Consider legitimate explanations such as property sales or inheritance.

Escalate if material concerns remain unresolved.

### Q34. A customer receives money from multiple countries and transfers it to one beneficiary. What are your concerns?

Potential concerns include layering and pass-through activity.

Review sender identities, countries, beneficiary relationships, frequency, aggregate values, and economic purpose.

### Q35. A customer receives $200,000 and withdraws almost everything within 24 hours. Is this suspicious?

It is unusual and warrants review, but context matters.

Investigate the source, destination, purpose, counterparties, and customer profile.

### Q36. A customer is involved in frequent cryptocurrency-related transfers. What would you investigate?

Review source of funds, exchange platforms, counterparties, wallet information where available, transaction frequency, sanctions exposure, and consistency with the customer profile.

### Q37. A customer receives funds from a high-risk jurisdiction. Would you automatically escalate?

No. Geographical risk is one factor.

Assess the customer's connection to the jurisdiction, sender, beneficiary, purpose, source of funds, and supporting evidence.

### Q38. A customer provides an explanation, but the documents do not match the transaction amount. What would you do?

Compare the documents with the transaction amount, date, sender, and purpose.

Seek clarification where appropriate and escalate unresolved discrepancies.

### Q39. A customer has high transaction volume, but all transactions are below a monitoring threshold. What would you investigate?

Review aggregate values, timing, frequency, common counterparties, customer profile, and economic rationale.

Assess the complete pattern rather than individual transactions in isolation.

### Q40. A customer receives a large inheritance and begins investing. What would you do?

Assess the inheritance as a potential source of funds.

Review appropriate inheritance or probate documents and bank statements.

Determine whether the amount and subsequent activity fit the customer's profile.

## Client ABC Assessment

### Case Study: USD 500,000 Transaction Monitoring Alert

**Customer Profile**

| Attribute | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Age | 40 |
| Nationality | Danish |
| Residence | China |
| Employer | JPMC |
| Annual post-tax salary | USD 150,000 |
| Onboarding date | 5 January 2025 |
| Declared total deposits | USD 1,000,000 |

### Transaction History

| Date | Deposit | Origin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 January | USD 200,000 | Not specified |
| 12 January | USD 250,000 | Not specified |
| 18 January | USD 300,000 | Not specified |
| 30 January | USD 150,000 | Denmark |
| 5 February | USD 20,000 | Jersey |
| 20 February | USD 500,000 | Not specified |

**Other Activity:**

- Trading commenced on 15 February.
- Total withdrawals: USD 200,000.
- 12 trades in non-margin products.
- Alert triggered by USD 500,000 incoming transaction on 20 February.

### Key Calculations

| Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Total listed deposits | USD 1,420,000 |
| Declared deposits | USD 1,000,000 |
| Deposit discrepancy | USD 420,000 |
| Annual salary | USD 150,000 |
| Deposits / annual salary | Approximately 9.47 times |
| Deposits less withdrawals | USD 1,220,000 |

Note: Deposits less withdrawals do not necessarily represent the account balance because trading results, fees, timing, and asset values are unknown.

### Key Red Flags

1. Deposits materially exceed declared annual income.
2. USD 420,000 discrepancy between declared and listed deposits.
3. Source and purpose of the USD 500,000 incoming payment are unknown.
4. Denmark and Jersey transfers require further assessment.
5. Trading commenced shortly before the alert-triggering transaction.
6. USD 200,000 withdrawals require destination and purpose analysis.

### Additional Information Required

- Bank statements showing the origin of deposits.
- Sending-account ownership information.
- Details of senders and counterparties.
- Source-of-funds evidence for the USD 500,000 transaction.
- Source-of-wealth information.
- Reconciliation of the USD 420,000 discrepancy.
- Investment objectives and expected account activity.

### Recommended Disposition

**Decision: Escalate for Further Investigation.**

The available information is insufficient to reasonably explain the account funding.

The income-to-deposit discrepancy, inconsistent deposit totals, and unexplained USD 500,000 transaction warrant further investigation.

These facts do not independently establish money laundering. A SAR/STR should be considered only if the investigation supports the applicable reporting threshold.

### Interview-Ready Answer

"After reviewing Client ABC's profile and transaction activity, I would recommend escalating the alert for further investigation rather than closing it at this stage.

The customer declared annual post-tax income of USD 150,000, while the listed deposits total USD 1.42 million, approximately 9.47 times annual income.

I also identified a USD 420,000 discrepancy between the stated total deposits and the listed transactions.

The USD 500,000 incoming transaction requires investigation into its source, sender, and purpose.

I would review the Denmark and Jersey transfers, reconcile the deposit discrepancy, assess the withdrawals, and determine whether the trading activity is consistent with the customer's profile.

I would seek appropriate bank statements and source-of-funds/source-of-wealth evidence.

Until the discrepancies and funding are satisfactorily explained, I would escalate. Any SAR/STR decision would follow the applicable jurisdictional threshold and internal process."

## Section 7 – SAR Narrative Writing

### Q42. What information should be included in a SAR narrative?

A SAR narrative should be factual, chronological, objective, and evidence-based.

Include:

- Subject and account details.
- Relevant transaction dates.
- Amounts and counterparties.
- Customer profile.
- Identified red flags.
- Research and enquiries performed.
- Customer explanations.
- Supporting documents.
- Reasons for suspicion.

### Q43. Write a Sample SAR Narrative for Client ABC.

**Subject:** Suspicious Account Funding Activity – Client ABC

**Customer Information:**

Client ABC is a 40-year-old Danish national residing in China. The customer was onboarded on 5 January 2025 and declared employment with JPMC, with annual post-tax salary of USD 150,000.

**Period and Activity:**

Between 10 January and 20 February 2025, six deposits were listed:

- USD 200,000 on 10 January.
- USD 250,000 on 12 January.
- USD 300,000 on 18 January.
- USD 150,000 on 30 January, originating from Denmark.
- USD 20,000 on 5 February, originating from Jersey.
- USD 500,000 on 20 February.

The final transaction triggered the monitoring alert.

Trading reportedly commenced on 15 February, with 12 non-margin trades and total withdrawals of USD 200,000.

**Reason for Concern:**

Listed deposits total USD 1.42 million, approximately 9.47 times the declared annual post-tax salary.

The customer profile records total deposits of USD 1 million, creating a USD 420,000 discrepancy.

The source and purpose of the USD 500,000 incoming payment have not been established from the information provided.

**Investigation Findings:**

The observed funding is materially higher than the declared employment income.

The deposit discrepancy and international funding activity require reconciliation and explanation.

Further information is needed to establish the source of funds, origin of payments, and economic purpose.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the information currently available, the account funding remains unexplained.

The income-to-deposit discrepancy, inconsistent deposit totals, and unexplained USD 500,000 payment warrant escalation and consideration of SAR/STR reporting in accordance with applicable requirements.

_This is an illustrative draft, not a completed report based on verified evidence. Do not include investigation steps, customer responses, or document findings that have not actually been established._

## Section 8 – Financial Markets and Investment Knowledge

### Q44. What are Financial Markets?

Financial markets are systems or venues where individuals and institutions buy and sell financial instruments such as shares, bonds, currencies, and derivatives.

### Q45. What are Financial Instruments?

Examples include:

- Equities.
- Bonds.
- Foreign exchange.
- Commodities.
- Futures.
- Options.
- CFDs.
- ETFs.

### Q46. What is the difference between Margin and Non-Margin Trading?

**Margin Trading:** Uses borrowed funds or leverage to increase market exposure.

**Non-Margin Trading:** Generally involves buying instruments using available funds without borrowing through margin.

### Q47. What is an ETF?

An Exchange-Traded Fund is an investment fund holding a basket of assets, such as shares or bonds, that trades on an exchange.

### Q48. What is a CFD?

A Contract for Difference is a derivative through which an investor speculates on price movements without owning the underlying asset.

### Q49. Why is understanding financial instruments important for a Transaction Monitoring Analyst?

Product knowledge helps analysts distinguish legitimate investment activity from potentially suspicious transactions.

It reduces the risk of misinterpreting normal investment activity.

Follow standard procedures, document decisions clearly, communicate effectively, and account for local regulatory differences.

## Part 3 – Mastercard, Visa and Payment Ecosystem

### 16\. What is your understanding of Mastercard and Visa in transaction monitoring?

Mastercard and Visa operate global payment networks facilitating card-payment authorisation, clearing, and settlement.

### 17\. What is the difference between an Issuer and an Acquirer?

- **Issuer:** Financial institution providing the payment card to the cardholder.
- **Acquirer:** Financial institution providing merchant payment acceptance services.

### 18\. What is a Merchant, Payment Processor and Payment Facilitator?

- **Merchant:** Sells goods or services.
- **Payment Processor:** Provides technology and processing services.
- **Payment Facilitator:** Enables sub-merchants to accept payments under an acquiring relationship.

### 19\. What merchant risks might trigger additional review?

- Unexplained spikes in transaction volume.
- Unusual refund or chargeback patterns.
- Activity inconsistent with the stated business.
- Suspicious geographical patterns.
- Undisclosed business activity.
- Unexplained settlement flows.

### 20\. What are Chargebacks and Refunds?

A refund is a merchant-initiated return of funds.

A chargeback is a cardholder dispute processed through the issuer and network rules.

### 21\. What would you check if a merchant's transaction volume suddenly increased?

Compare current volume with historical activity, business model, seasonality, average ticket size, geography, refund rates, and settlement details.

### 22\. What is the difference between Authorisation, Clearing and Settlement?

- **Authorisation:** Approval or decline of a transaction.
- **Clearing:** Exchange and reconciliation of transaction details.
- **Settlement:** Movement of funds between relevant financial institutions.

## Part 5 – Red Flags and Financial Crime Typologies

### 31\. Explain Structuring.

Splitting transactions into smaller amounts to avoid detection or reporting requirements.

### 32\. Explain Layering.

Moving funds through multiple transactions, accounts, entities, or jurisdictions to obscure their origin or ownership.

### 33\. What are Round-Dollar Payments?

Transactions in whole or rounded amounts, such as USD 10,000 or USD 50,000.

They are not inherently suspicious.

### 34\. What are High-Risk Jurisdictions?

Jurisdictions presenting higher financial crime risks due to factors such as sanctions, weak AML controls, corruption, or FATF public statements.

### 35\. What is Unusual Payment-Pattern Analysis?

Comparing actual payment behaviour with expected activity to identify anomalies in values, frequency, timing, counterparties, geography, or purpose.

### 36\. What is Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML)?

Using trade transactions to disguise criminal proceeds or move value.

Potential indicators include:

- Over-invoicing.
- Under-invoicing.
- Multiple invoicing.
- Misdescription of goods.
- Unusual shipping routes.
- Inconsistent trade documents.

### 37\. How would you investigate suspected TBML?

Identify buyers, sellers, intermediaries, goods, shipping routes, invoice values, payment flows, and inconsistencies in supporting documents.

### 38\. What is a Pass-Through Account?

An account that receives funds and transfers them onward, often quickly, with little apparent economic activity retained.

## Part 6 – RFI, Documentation, Quality and Operational Controls

### 39\. What is an RFI and when would you raise one?

An RFI is a Request for Information used to obtain clarification or supporting evidence needed to understand activity.

### 40\. What makes a good RFI?

It should be specific, relevant, proportionate, and understandable.

### 41\. How do you validate documents submitted by a customer?

Check relevance, completeness, consistency, names, dates, amounts, account details, and counterparties.

### 42\. How do you handle confidential customer information?

Access only information required for the assigned task, use approved systems, and follow data protection and information security policies.

### 43\. How do you balance productivity and quality?

Prioritise according to risk, urgency, and SLA requirements while maintaining mandatory investigation standards.

### 44\. What would you do if you noticed a recurring process issue?

Document examples, assess impact, raise the issue through the appropriate channel, and suggest controlled improvements.

## 2\. Transaction Monitoring – Customer and Payment Scenarios

### Scenario 1. A salaried customer earning USD 60,000 annually receives USD 400,000 from an unrelated individual.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Customer profile assessment and third-party funding investigation.

**Approach:**

- Review the customer profile.
- Identify the sender and relationship.
- Establish the payment purpose.
- Investigate the source of funds.
- Review supporting evidence.
- Assess whether the activity is consistent with the profile.

**Model Answer:**

"I would treat the payment as unusual because it is materially higher than the customer's declared income and comes from an unrelated party. I would establish who sent it, why the customer received it, where the funds originated, and whether the payment is supported by evidence. If material questions remain unresolved, I would escalate."

### Scenario 2. A customer receives funds from five unrelated people and sends the total to one overseas beneficiary on the same day.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Pass-through activity and layering.

**Approach:** Map incoming and outgoing transactions, identify all counterparties, review timing, and assess economic purpose.

**Model Answer:**

"This pattern may indicate pass-through activity because funds from multiple unrelated parties are consolidated and transferred onward quickly. I would review the senders, beneficiary, relationships, payment purpose, and overall economic rationale. If the flow cannot be reasonably explained, I would escalate."

### Scenario 3. A customer deposits several amounts just below an internal monitoring threshold.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Structuring awareness.

**Approach:** Review aggregate values, timing, frequency, linked accounts, and customer profile.

**Model Answer:**

"I would assess whether the transactions are linked and whether the pattern appears designed to avoid controls. Amounts below a threshold are not automatically suspicious, so I would review the complete pattern and document the rationale."

### Scenario 4. A customer receives a large payment and transfers nearly all of it out within two hours.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Rapid movement of funds.

**Approach:** Build a chronological transaction flow, identify counterparties, verify ownership, and review purpose.

**Model Answer:**

"The rapid movement is a potential red flag, particularly if the account retains little balance and no clear purpose is evident. I would trace the incoming and outgoing payments and compare the activity with the customer's normal account usage."

### Scenario 5. A customer says a USD 250,000 credit came from selling a property but provides only a brief email.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Evidence assessment.

**Approach:** Request appropriate supporting documents, compare transaction details, and assess consistency.

**Model Answer:**

"The email provides an explanation but may not sufficiently evidence the source of funds. I would compare the details with the payment and request appropriate supporting documents, such as a sale agreement or completion statement."

### Scenario 6. A customer's account is funded by salary, then suddenly receives large cash deposits.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Profile-based monitoring.

**Approach:** Review occupation, cash exposure, deposit locations, frequency, and source of funds.

**Model Answer:**

"I would investigate the change from salary-based funding to large cash deposits. I would assess whether the customer's occupation or business could reasonably generate cash and whether the activity is supported by a credible explanation."

### Scenario 7. A customer receives repeated payments from a high-risk jurisdiction but says they are family remittances.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Risk-based judgement.

**Approach:** Identify senders, relationships, purpose, source of funds, and applicable sanctions risks.

**Model Answer:**

"I would not automatically treat the payments as suspicious solely because of the jurisdiction. I would assess the customer's connection to the country, sender identity, relationship, source of funds, purpose, and transaction pattern."

### Scenario 8. A customer receives multiple payments from their own verified bank account.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Own-account transfers versus third-party funding.

**Approach:** Verify ownership and trace original funding where required.

**Model Answer:**

"If the sending account genuinely belongs to the same customer and transfers are consistent with their profile, this may be a reasonable explanation. I would still check the original source of funds where required."

### Scenario 9. A customer receives a large inheritance and invests it shortly afterwards.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Source of funds and source of wealth.

**Approach:** Establish inheritance origin, review relevant documents, and assess investment activity.

**Model Answer:**

"I would assess the inheritance as a potential legitimate source of funds and understand how it fits into the customer's overall wealth. If the evidence supports the amount and investment activity fits the profile, the alert may be closed with a documented rationale."

### Scenario 10. Declared total deposits are USD 1 million, but transaction records show USD 1.42 million.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Numerical accuracy and reconciliation.

**Approach:** Recalculate transactions, review date ranges, duplicates, reversals, and internal transfers.

**Model Answer:**

"I would reconcile the underlying transactions, confirm the review period, and establish whether the stated figure is gross deposits or another measure. The USD 420,000 difference is material and should be resolved before reaching a final conclusion."

## 3\. KYC, Sanctions, PEP and Adverse Media Scenarios

### Scenario 11. A sanctions screening alert matches the customer's name, but the date of birth differs.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Potential-match handling.

**Approach:** Compare DOB, nationality, address, aliases, identification numbers, and associated entities.

**Model Answer:**

"A name similarity alone does not establish a true match. I would compare all available identifiers and follow the screening procedure. If it remains a potential match, I would escalate promptly to the sanctions team."

### Scenario 12. A customer is identified as a PEP but has low transaction volume.

**What the interviewer is testing:** PEP risk assessment.

**Approach:** Confirm PEP status, assess role and jurisdiction, review source of wealth and funds, and apply required EDD.

**Model Answer:**

"PEP status does not mean the customer is involved in crime. I would apply the required enhanced due diligence and risk assessment. Monitoring should be proportionate to the customer's overall risk, not volume alone."

### Scenario 13. Adverse media reports that a person with the customer's name was arrested for fraud.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Identity resolution and source reliability.

**Approach:** Compare identifiers, assess publisher and date, and establish the status of the allegation.

**Model Answer:**

"I would not assume it is the customer based on the name or treat an arrest as proof of guilt. I would compare identifiers, assess the publisher and date, understand the allegation and its status, and document my findings."

### Scenario 14. A customer is connected to a sanctioned company through ownership.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Ownership and control assessment.

**Approach:** Map the ownership structure, verify information, apply the relevant sanctions regime, and escalate.

**Model Answer:**

"I would map the ownership and control structure and verify the information using reliable sources. I would apply the relevant sanctions regime and internal rules rather than relying on a generic ownership threshold."

### Scenario 15. A customer refuses to provide source-of-funds documents.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Missing information and escalation.

**Approach:** Document the request, follow up, assess available information, and escalate unresolved gaps.

**Model Answer:**

"I would follow the approved request and follow-up process and document the customer's response or non-response. If a material gap remains, I would escalate. I would not disclose confidential SAR information."

## 4\. Merchant, Card-Network and Payment Scenarios

### Scenario 16. A merchant declares clothing sales, but its website and transactions suggest electronics sales.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Merchant due diligence.

**Approach:** Compare onboarding information, website content, transaction data, and business activity.

**Model Answer:**

"I would check whether the merchant's declared business accurately reflects its current activity. If there is a legitimate business change, I would establish whether the merchant profile has been updated. An unexplained mismatch should be escalated."

### Scenario 17. A merchant's transaction volume increases by 400% in one week.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Anomaly analysis.

**Approach:** Compare historical volume, seasonality, average ticket size, geography, refunds, and chargebacks.

**Model Answer:**

"I would validate the data and compare it with the merchant's historical pattern. I would check for promotions, seasonal events, or new sales channels. If the increase remains unexplained, I would escalate."

### Scenario 18. A merchant has an unusually high refund rate and repeated refunds to different cards.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Refund abuse and card misuse.

**Approach:** Review original transactions, refund methods, timing, amounts, and chargebacks.

**Model Answer:**

"I would examine whether refunds are linked to genuine original purchases and whether they are being returned through the permitted method. If refunds appear unrelated to original transactions or breach controls, I would escalate."

### Scenario 19. A merchant processes payments for another business not disclosed at onboarding.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Undisclosed third-party processing.

**Approach:** Identify the actual seller, products, settlement beneficiary, and contractual arrangements.

**Model Answer:**

"I would determine who is actually selling the goods or services and who receives the settlement funds. I would compare this with the approved business model and relevant network rules."

### Scenario 20. A customer disputes a card transaction and claims it is fraud. Is this automatically an AML case?

**What the interviewer is testing:** Fraud versus AML.

**Approach:** Follow the appropriate fraud or chargeback process and assess whether broader suspicious activity exists.

**Model Answer:**

"No. A card dispute is primarily handled through the relevant fraud or chargeback process. However, if the investigation reveals broader suspicious patterns, I would raise those concerns through the appropriate AML escalation channel."

### Scenario 21. Explain a case where issuer, acquirer, and network roles matter.

**What the interviewer is testing:** Payment ecosystem knowledge.

**Model Answer:**

"The issuer provides the card to the cardholder, the acquirer provides merchant acceptance services, and the card network supports transaction routing and network rules. A processor may provide technical processing services."

**Approach:** Match invoice numbers, dates, values, goods, shipment IDs, and payment records.

**Model Answer:**

## Important Interview Advice

Do not simply memorise definitions.

Practise explaining:

1. Why a transaction is unusual.
2. What additional information you would seek.
3. How you would assess the customer's explanation.
4. What evidence supports your decision.
5. Why you would close or escalate the alert.

A strong Transaction Monitoring Analyst does not automatically escalate every large transaction or close every transaction supported by a customer explanation.

Decisions should be:

- Evidence-based.
- Risk-sensitive.
- Consistent with applicable policies.
- Clearly documented.
- Defensible during quality reviews and audits.

**Core Message:**

The strongest interview answer is not the one containing the most AML terminology. It is the one that identifies the risk, explains the evidence needed, tests the customer's explanation, reaches a proportionate decision, and documents the investigation clearly.
